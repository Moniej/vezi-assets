# Stage 14 — Formal Factor Specification for the Two Cleared News Signals

**Date:** 2026-08-08 (amended twice, 2026-08-08, following two rounds of independent review — see §14K, §14L)
**Status: PRE-REGISTRATION. No backtest has been run against this specification. No parameter in this document was chosen by observing returns.**
**Scope: two families only** — Governance/Management-Change (GMC) and Corporate-Identity Restructuring (CIR). Earnings/profitability and recapitalization/capital-actions are explicitly out of scope for this document and must not be added without a new, separately-authorized stage. No H-019 created. ## 14A. Formal event definitions

### Family 1 — Governance / Management-Change (GMC)

- **Qualifying event types** (taxonomy leaf, `configs/event_taxonomy.toml`, `[corporate]` category): `management_change` only.
  - Qualifying disclosure content, all of which must name a specific individual and a specific board/executive role: board member resignation; board member appointment; chairman election (AGM or board vote); MD/CEO appointment, confirmation, or resignation; executive director appointment or resignation.
- **Non-qualifying event types**: routine board-meeting notices that do not themselves disclose a personnel change (agenda-only); management commentary/quotes embedded within an earnings or capital-raise article (the commentary is not itself a GMC event — see also §14A CIR non-qualifying list for the same principle); staff-level hiring below board/executive-director rank; compensation or remuneration disclosures with no accompanying personnel change; a mere mention that a manager "will proceed prudently" or similar forward-looking quote with no structural change.
- **Minimum evidence requirement**: the source article must state, in its own text (not inferred), (a) the individual's name, (b) the specific role, and (c) either an effective date or an announcement/disclosure date. An article that only says "changes are expected" without naming a person or role does not qualify — mark UNKNOWN, do not wait-and-guess.
- **Source requirements**: Nairametrics or MarketForces Africa only (the two sources cleared through Stage 10A-13). Any other outlet is out of scope until separately authorized, per the standing instruction against adding sources.
- **Event-date definition**: the disclosed **effective date** of the personnel change if explicitly stated (e.g. "effective April 1, 2026"); if no effective date is stated, the event-date is the **announced_date** (see below) itself, never inferred or backdated.
- **Publication/knowledge-date definition**: the article's own publication dateline (Nairametrics: parsed from the URL's `/YYYY/MM/DD/` path or article body; MarketForces: taken from the fetched article's stated publication date only — MFA URLs carry no date, so an MFA article with no stated in-body date cannot be dated and is UNKNOWN, not estimated).
- **Ticker mapping rule**: the company named in the personnel-change disclosure must resolve to exactly one H-011-universe ticker via `securities.ticker`. An article naming a subsidiary or an unlisted affiliate does not map to the parent's ticker unless the article explicitly states the disclosure was filed under the listed entity's NGX disclosure obligations.
- **Duplicate-event rule**: see §14D — canonical key is `(ticker, event_type='management_change', effective_date_or_announced_date, named_individual_role)`.
- **Multi-outlet rule**: see §14D.
- **Correction/restatement rule**: if a later article states the same individual/role/date but corrects a detail (e.g. a spelling correction, a corrected effective date), it is a **RESTATEMENT** of the same `event_uid`, handled by `event_pipeline.validate_batch()`'s existing uid-restatement path (append-only, `events_asof()` reads the latest `as_of_date`) — never a new event and never an overwrite. If a later article reports a genuinely different subsequent action (e.g. the newly appointed director later resigns), that is a **new, distinct** GMC event, not a restatement.
- **PIT eligibility rule**: see §14E — GMC events are eligible immediately once the announced_date and named individual/role are confirmed; no figure-integrity waiting period applies (this family carries no numeric-fact risk).
- **UNKNOWN handling**: any GMC candidate failing the minimum-evidence requirement, or with an unresolvable ticker mapping, or with no source-confirmed date, is marked `direction=unknown`/`PIT_status=UNKNOWN` and **excluded from the eligible dataset** (§14H) until resolved — never defaulted into the dataset with a guessed value.

### Family 2 — Corporate-Identity Restructuring (CIR)

- **Qualifying event types**: `corporate_restructuring`, `merger`, `ownership_change` (all existing `[corporate]` taxonomy leaves).
  - Qualifying disclosure content: a shareholder/board-approved company rename; a disclosed sector or business-model pivot approved at AGM/board level; a merger or business-combination announcement or completion; an externally-disclosed acquisition of a **substantial shareholding** (defined here, consistent with NGX/SEC substantial-shareholding disclosure convention, as ≥5% of issued share capital acquired in a single disclosed transaction or a stated cumulative stake reaching ≥5%).
- **Non-qualifying event types**: office relocation alone, with no accompanying structural/identity change (explicitly excluded — ROYALEX's 2025-02-13 relocation article, found in Stage 13, does **not** qualify under this stricter definition, a deliberate narrowing disclosed here); routine AGM approval of ordinary business (accounts, auditor re-appointment) with no restructuring content; a stake acquisition below the 5% substantial-shareholding threshold, or one whose size is not disclosed (mark UNKNOWN, do not assume it clears the threshold); delisting/suspension events (these belong to a `regulatory_action`/`suspension` taxonomy branch, not CIR, and remain out of scope for this stage).
- **Minimum evidence requirement**: the source article must state, in its own text, the specific structural change (new name; specific merger counterparty; specific acquirer and stake size) and either a shareholder/board approval date or a transaction/completion date. A rumor or unconfirmed report ("sources say," "rumoured") does not qualify — mark UNKNOWN (this mirrors the platform's own prior handling of DEAPCAP's 2020 "rumoured AMCON takeover" article, correctly excluded in Stage 13).
- **Source requirements**: same as GMC — Nairametrics or MarketForces Africa only.
- **Event-date definition**: for shareholder/board-approved actions (renames, pivots, merger approvals), the **AGM/board resolution date**; for market-transacted stake acquisitions, the **transaction/disclosure date** as stated (e.g. ROYALEX/Nexamont: 2025-09-21, the article's own dateline, since no separate transaction date was disclosed in that article's text).
- **Publication/knowledge-date definition**: same rule as GMC.
- **Ticker mapping rule**: same rule as GMC. For mergers involving a non-listed counterparty (e.g. LEGENDINT/Spectranet), the event maps only to the listed party's ticker; the unlisted counterparty is recorded in `notes`, never given a synthetic ticker.
- **Duplicate-event rule**: canonical key `(ticker, event_type ∈ {corporate_restructuring, merger, ownership_change}, event_date, structural-change-description-hash)` — see §14D.
- **Multi-outlet rule**: see §14D.
- **Correction/restatement rule**: same principle as GMC — a corrected detail of the *same* structural action is a restatement (shared `event_uid`); a *subsequent, distinct* structural action (e.g. a merger completes after having earlier only been announced) is a **new** event, explicitly linked in `notes` to the prior `event_uid` as its successor, never merged into one row.
- **PIT eligibility rule**: identical to GMC — CIR events carry no numeric-fact risk and are eligible immediately once minimum evidence is confirmed.
- **UNKNOWN handling**: identical principle to GMC — fails closed, excluded from the eligible dataset, never guessed.

## 14B. Signal construction (conceptual structure, no parameters fitted)

```
event occurrence (real-world action: appointment / rename / merger / stake acquisition)
   → article discovery (Nairametrics or MarketForces Africa, within the approved-source constraint)
   → event classification (§14A qualifying/non-qualifying test — deterministic, evidence-gated)
   → canonical event identity assigned (§14D — natural key, shared across outlets)
   → direction assignment (§14C — objective rule applied to the structured event fields, NOT to any LLM-generated sentiment)
   → information date established (§14E — knowledge_timestamp, conservative session boundary)
   → PIT eligibility check (§14E — PIT-SAFE required; PIT-UNCERTAIN or PIT-FAILED excluded)
   → signal activation (eligible_from = the §14E `eligible_from` algorithm applied to knowledge_timestamp alone — a function of publication date and the trading calendar only, never of the event's own effective date, per §14E and §14I's explicit no-lookahead rule)
   → signal expiration (eligible_until — see below; no holding period is assumed at this stage)
```

**No holding period is assumed.** Per the explicit instruction, this stage defines only when a signal becomes *eligible* (`eligible_from`) and, separately, a data-integrity expiration (`eligible_until`) that bounds how long an observation may be treated as "the latest known state" for a given ticker/event-type before a research process must re-check for a subsequent restatement or successor event — this is a **data-freshness** boundary, not a trading holding-period assumption. `eligible_until` is defined as: unbounded (open-ended) unless and until a successor event (§14A correction/restatement rule) supersedes it, at which point the prior observation's `eligible_until` is set to the successor's `knowledge_timestamp`. No decay window, half-life, or holding-period parameter is specified — that would be a performance-adjacent choice this stage is explicitly barred from making.

Where direction cannot be established objectively from the structured event fields (see §14C), the event is marked `direction=unknown` and, per the explicit instruction, **is not force-classified positive/negative** — it may still be eligible for the dataset (§14H) as an `unknown`-direction observation, but cannot be used to form a directional signal.

## 14C. Directional taxonomy — objective, non-subjective rules

The platform already has an enforced, schema-level direction vocabulary: `events.direction CHECK (direction IN ('bullish','bearish','neutral','unknown'))` (`schema/schema.sql`), identical in shape to `vocab.DIRECTIONS`. This specification reuses that existing vocabulary directly — **POSITIVE = bullish, NEGATIVE = bearish, NEUTRAL = neutral, UNKNOWN = unknown** — rather than inventing a parallel one, per the standing instruction to use existing structures wherever possible.

**Critical rule, stated explicitly per the instruction**: `investment_implications.direction` (the Gemini-derived, `UNREVIEWED_LLM_CONFIDENCE_FLOOR`-capped subjective assessment) is **never** read as the signal's direction. The signal's direction is derived **only** from the structured `events` row's own fields, using the following deterministic rule table:

**GMC direction rules**:
| Structured condition | Direction |
|---|---|
| A new chairman/MD/CEO/executive director is appointed or confirmed, with no simultaneous resignation-under-controversy disclosed in the same article | **NEUTRAL** (a personnel change alone has no objectively determinable sign without a subjective quality judgment this specification refuses to make) |
| A resignation is disclosed with an explicit stated reason indicating **regulatory/compliance/governance concern** (e.g. removed for cause, regulatory disqualification) | **NEGATIVE** |
| A resignation is disclosed as **routine/voluntary** with no stated adverse reason (e.g. "resigned to pursue other interests," or no reason given) | **NEUTRAL** |
| Any GMC event where the above conditions cannot be objectively determined from the article's stated text | **UNKNOWN** |

Applying this table to the confirmed Stage 11-13 GMC examples: SUNUASSUR's Bakare resignation (no adverse reason stated) → NEUTRAL. Adaramola's appointment → NEUTRAL. UNIVINSURE's Duru CEO confirmation → NEUTRAL. VERITASKAP's Irukera chairman election → NEUTRAL. **Every confirmed GMC event in the current corpus resolves to NEUTRAL under this objective rule** — this is disclosed plainly, not smoothed over: the objective rule, correctly applied, does not currently produce any POSITIVE or NEGATIVE GMC observations in-sample. That is a finding about the rule's strictness applied to real data, not a flaw to be loosened to manufacture directional variance.

**CIR direction rules**:
| Structured condition | Direction |
|---|---|
| A merger/combination is announced with a stated **increase** in combined capital base/market value versus either party's standalone prior state (as stated in the article, not computed) | **POSITIVE** |
| A rename/sector pivot is disclosed with an accompanying **disclosed capital injection** from a new strategic investor in the same transaction | **POSITIVE** |
| An external party's substantial-shareholding acquisition is disclosed with **no stated strategic intent** and no accompanying capital-raise/injection | **NEUTRAL** (ownership change alone, absent stated intent, has no objectively determinable sign) |
| A restructuring is disclosed as a **response to** a disclosed adverse condition (e.g. explicit distress, regulatory non-compliance, forced sale) | **NEGATIVE** |
| Any CIR event where the above conditions cannot be objectively determined | **UNKNOWN** |

Applying this to confirmed examples: DEAPCAP's rename + Banklink Africa/RGM capital injection → **POSITIVE** (disclosed capital injection accompanying the pivot). LEGENDINT/Spectranet merger, stated N80bn combined capital base vs. LEGENDINT's own prior ~N12bn market cap → **POSITIVE** (stated increase). ROYALEX/Nexamont stake acquisition, no stated strategic intent in the source article ("has not yet disclosed its strategic intentions") → **NEUTRAL** under the objective rule, explicitly **not** guessed positive or negative despite the stock's prior 234% run mentioned in the same article (price action is not a permitted input to this rule — see §14F).

## 14D. Event deduplication — canonical identity, built on existing architecture

**No new deduplication mechanism is introduced for within-source handling.** This specification reuses `event_pipeline.validate_batch()` exactly as fixed and regression-tested in Stage 10E, and reconfirmed under real multi-type production load in Stages 11-12. **Cross-outlet linkage, however, required an additional explicit procedure — see the correction below.**

- **Canonical key** (the natural key already enforced by `validate_batch()`'s vs-DB matching logic, `event_pipeline.py:150-153`): `(event_type, announced_date, scope='ticker', ticker)` — 4 columns for ticker-scoped rows. Note for precision: the *within-batch* dedup key (`event_pipeline.py:132`) additionally carries `index_code`, making it 5 columns in general, but `index_code` is always NULL for `scope='ticker'` rows (GMC/CIR are always ticker-scoped), so the two keys are equivalent in this specification's actual use — stated exactly here since the original draft imprecisely called the 4-column vs-DB key "the same 5-column key," which a careful implementer could have misread as requiring `index_code` to be populated.
- **Within-source restatement/duplicate handling**: unchanged, exactly as Stage 10E fixed and Stage 12 reconfirmed — same-source resubmission of an identical payload is `REJECT`ed; a same-source resubmission with a changed payload is a `RESTATEMENT` sharing the same `event_uid`.

**⚠ Correction following independent review (2026-08-08): cross-outlet event linkage is NOT pipeline-enforced and requires an explicit procedure, not an assumption.** `event_uid` (`schema.sql:214-217`) is documented as a "stable **source-assigned** ID" — it is supplied by whoever builds the ingestion batch, not generated or matched by the pipeline. `validate_batch()`'s `same_src` branch (`event_pipeline.py:157`) only performs uid-based restatement/reject logic when the new row's source name matches an *existing* row's source; for a genuine second-outlet submission, the code falls only to the `CONFLICT` check (`effective_date`/`direction` agreement), which can prevent silent overwriting but does **not** establish that two rows describe the same underlying event, and does **not** assign them a shared `event_uid`. The original version of this specification asserted `duplicate_status` was "a deterministic function of already-stored fields" — that was not accurate; cross-outlet identity is not stored anywhere unless a human deliberately makes it so. This section now specifies exactly how:

**⚠ Second correction (2026-08-08, from a follow-up review of the first correction above): the matching query below must include §14A's identity-distinguishing field, not only `(ticker, event_type, date window)`.** The first correction, as originally written, matched only on ticker/event_type/date — which is **not** §14A's actual canonical key (GMC's key additionally includes the named individual's role; CIR's includes the structural-change description). A ticker/type/date-only match can find "exactly one existing row" for two genuinely *different* events (e.g. one outlet reporting a chairman's resignation, another reporting a different director's unrelated appointment at the same company two days later) and would have **mandated** merging them under the "exactly one match → reuse the uid" rule — silently conflating two distinct events, in direct contradiction of §14A's own instruction that a distinct action is "a new, distinct event, not a restatement." This is fixed below by adding the identity field to the match condition itself, so a ticker/type/date match on a *different* individual or structural change no longer counts as "exactly one match" — it becomes a **zero-identity-match** case (new uid) unless the identity fields are also judged the same.

**⚠ Disclosed limitation, not fixed further (2026-08-08, third review round): "identity content agreement" is itself a judgment call, not a mechanical string-match, and this document does not resolve that.** A third review correctly pointed out that "Chairman John Doe resigns" and "Board member J. Doe steps down" describe the same person under different phrasing — no rule stated here distinguishes exact-string matching, normalized-name matching, or human/LLM-judged equivalence. This specification does **not** attempt to resolve that ambiguity mechanically; it is disclosed here, explicitly, as a second human-judgment point in the pipeline alongside §14C's already-disclosed direction-classification judgment (§14J item 2). The practical consequence is stated plainly: identity-content comparison in the ingestion-time and dataset-build-time procedures above must be performed by a human (or an LLM assist whose output a human confirms) applying ordinary judgment about whether two descriptions name the same person/role or the same structural change — not by an automated exact-match. Where that judgment is not confident, the fail-closed rule already stated above applies: treat as ambiguous, resolve manually, never auto-merge and never auto-split.

**⚠ Fifth correction (2026-08-08, from a provenance investigation following Round 4's hostile review): "two different `source_id`s" is not a usable test against the real database and is replaced below with outlet derivation from `source_url`.** Round 4 found, and a follow-up investigation (`docs/STAGE14_ROUND4_BLOCKER_INVESTIGATION_2026-08-08.md`) confirmed by live query, that every news `events`/`documents` row ingested by this project — both outlets, every stage — shares exactly one `source_id` (`16`, `stage10c_news_pilot`, self-documented as a provisional placeholder registration since Stage 10C). `source_id` therefore cannot distinguish Nairametrics from MarketForces Africa and never could; the mechanism as originally specified was unreachable. Outlet identity is not, however, missing from the data — it is present, complete, and already stored in `source_url` on every relevant row (confirmed 100% populated across all 13 news `events` rows and 18 news `documents` rows checked in the investigation). This correction replaces the source_id-based test with a deterministic derivation from that existing field. **No historical `source_id` value is altered by this correction** — this is a read-time rule for the dedup procedure only, not a data change.

**Outlet-domain normalization procedure** (exact, deterministic):
1. Take the candidate row's `source_url`.
2. If `source_url` is `NULL`, empty, or fails to parse as a well-formed URL (no extractable hostname): outlet is **UNKNOWN**. A row with UNKNOWN outlet can never participate in cross-outlet corroboration matching (it cannot be confirmed as a "different outlet" from anything) — it is evaluated only for within-source dedup (§14D's unchanged, pipeline-enforced mechanism above) and otherwise treated as its own unlinked `primary` observation. This is a fail-closed default, not an error that halts ingestion.
3. Otherwise, extract the hostname using **`urlparse(source_url).hostname`, not `.netloc`** — this distinction is stated explicitly following a sixth review round: `.netloc` includes a port number if present (`urlparse("https://nairametrics.com:443/x").netloc == "nairametrics.com:443"`), which would fail step 5's exact/suffix match and silently misclassify a real, approved-outlet URL as UNKNOWN merely for carrying a port; `.hostname` excludes the port and normalizes case, avoiding this. Lower-case the result (`.hostname` already does this in Python's `urllib.parse`, but state it explicitly here so the rule doesn't depend on a specific library's default behavior).
4. Strip a leading `www.` if present (e.g. `www.nairametrics.com` → `nairametrics.com`), since this platform's own real data already shows the same outlet's URLs both with and without a `www.`/subdomain prefix (`nairametrics.com` and `stocks.nairametrics.com` both occur in this corpus) — see step 5 for how subdomains are handled.
5. **Outlet/domain mapping** (the only two approved sources, per §14A/§14I): any hostname equal to `nairametrics.com`, or ending in `.nairametrics.com` (covering observed subdomains such as `stocks.nairametrics.com`), maps to outlet `nairametrics`. Any hostname equal to `dmarketforces.com`, or ending in `.dmarketforces.com`, maps to outlet `marketforces_africa`. Any other hostname — including a plausible-looking but unrecognized domain, a typo, or a redirect target — maps to outlet **UNKNOWN** and is handled exactly as step 2 (fail closed, no cross-outlet matching, evaluated only as its own unlinked observation). This is a strict allow-list, not a best-effort guess: an unrecognized domain is never assumed to be one of the two approved outlets by default, consistent with §14A's existing rule that only these two sources are eligible at all.
6. Two rows are considered **different outlets** for the purposes of the matching rule below if and only if step 5 resolves them to two different, both-non-UNKNOWN outlet values. Two UNKNOWN-outlet rows are never treated as "different outlets" (there is nothing to compare), nor as "the same outlet" — they simply do not participate in cross-outlet matching at all.

- **Ingestion-time rule (preferred path)**: before submitting a batch containing what may be a second outlet's report of an already-known event, the ingesting party **must** query existing `events` rows for `(ticker, event_type)` with `announced_date` within a **±3 calendar day window** (provisional — see caveat below) of the candidate row's own `announced_date`, **and** compare the candidate's disclosed identity content (for GMC: the named individual and role; for CIR: the specific structural change described — new name, merger counterparty, or acquirer identity) against each date/ticker/type match. Only a row that matches on **ticker, event_type, date window, AND identity content** counts as a match. If exactly one such row matches, its `event_uid` **must** be reused verbatim for the new row (never a freshly-generated uid). If zero rows match on the full condition — including the case where a ticker/type/date match exists but describes a *different* individual/structural change — a **new** `event_uid` is assigned (a date/ticker/type match with a different identity is evidence of two distinct events, not ambiguity to resolve). If more than one row matches on the full condition (an identity collision, e.g. two articles both naming the same person/role within the window but with other details unresolved), the ingesting party must resolve the ambiguity manually before ingesting — this is a fail-closed rule, not a convenience default.
- **Dataset-build-time reconciliation (fallback, for rows already ingested without the above check having been applied)**: a dataset-build process **must** run the same full `(ticker, event_type, ±3-day window, identity content)` query as a candidate-matching pass over all eligible rows. Any pair of rows matching on **all four** components, resolving to two different (both non-UNKNOWN) **outlets by the domain-normalization procedure above**, and *not* already sharing an `event_uid`, is flagged `duplicate_status = candidate_corroboration_unconfirmed` — **not** silently merged, and **not** counted as two independent observations either. A ticker/type/date match with *differing* identity content is **not** flagged as a corroboration candidate at all — per the second correction above, it is treated as two independent `primary` events from the outset, since differing identity content is itself the disambiguating evidence. Rows genuinely flagged `candidate_corroboration_unconfirmed` are **excluded from any count used for §14F cross-sectional aggregation** until a human confirms them as either (a) the same event (re-tagged with a shared `event_uid`, one marked `corroborating`), or (b) genuinely distinct events (both retained as independent `primary` observations). This mirrors the fail-closed principle already used throughout §14A/§14E — an unresolved ambiguity is excluded, never guessed in either direction.

**Test cases for the corrected procedure** (traced by hand against the exact rules above, not run as code in this document):
1. *Same event, same outlet* (e.g. a Nairametrics article re-crawled or resubmitted): both rows resolve to outlet `nairametrics`; step 6 requires *different* outlets for corroboration-candidate status, so same-outlet rows are **never** treated as cross-outlet corroboration — they fall to the unchanged within-source `validate_batch()` path (identical payload → `REJECT`; changed payload → `RESTATEMENT`), exactly as before this correction.
2. *Same event, Nairametrics + MarketForces Africa*: `nairametrics.com` vs. `dmarketforces.com` resolve to two different, both-known outlets; if ticker/event_type/date-window/identity-content all also match, this **is** flagged `candidate_corroboration_unconfirmed` — a genuine corroboration candidate, correctly reachable now (it was not, under the old `source_id` test).
3. *Different events, same ticker/event_type, close dates* (e.g. the real SUNUASSUR case: a board resignation on 2026-04-11 and an unrelated rights-issue approval on 2026-02-11 — different `event_type` in this specific pair, but consider the closer hypothetical of two different personnel changes within the window): identity-content comparison (already required by the second correction, unchanged here) still governs — a ticker/type/date match with *differing* identity content is never auto-merged regardless of outlet, exactly as the second correction established; this correction only changes how "different outlet" is detected, not the identity-content requirement.
4. *Missing or ambiguous identity, or a malformed/missing `source_url`*: per step 2 above, a row with no parseable `source_url` cannot be confirmed as a different outlet from anything, so it is never merged; combined with the existing identity-content ambiguity rule, any case where identity or outlet cannot be confidently resolved falls to `candidate_corroboration_unconfirmed` (if a ticker/type/date match otherwise exists) or remains an unlinked `primary` observation (if it doesn't) — never a guessed merge.
5. *Unknown/unrecognized domain*: per step 5, resolves to outlet UNKNOWN and is excluded from cross-outlet matching entirely — fails closed, consistent with §14A's restriction to only the two approved sources.
- **⚠ The ±3-calendar-day window is provisional, not empirically derived.** No measurement of actual observed cross-outlet date lag exists in this corpus (the Stage 11-13 pilot did not process enough confirmed cross-outlet pairs to calibrate this number). ±3 days is a plausibility-based placeholder, stated here rather than hidden. Before this procedure is relied on at scale, it should be checked against whatever genuine cross-outlet pairs exist or arise (e.g. by comparing dates on any future confirmed same-event pair across the two sources) and tightened or widened accordingly, or explicitly re-affirmed as adequate — not left unexamined.
- **Stage 10E ticker-aware dedup compatibility — explicitly verified for the within-source case only**: the ticker-inclusive key is required precisely because both GMC and CIR are always `scope='ticker'` events (per taxonomy, `default_scope = "ticker"` for the `[corporate]` category) — meaning every within-source submission this specification produces is exactly the class of event Stage 10E's fix was built to correctly handle. This was re-confirmed working correctly via Stage 12's real production ingestion of 11 GMC/CIR-type events with 0 false rejections and 0 false collisions. **This verification covers within-source dedup only** — it does not, and was never claimed by Stage 12 to, cover cross-outlet linkage, which is a distinct mechanism specified fresh in this section.
- **Restatement vs. new-event distinction**: formalized in §14A per family; mechanically, a restatement shares `event_uid`, a new/successor event does not — this remains correct and unaffected by the correction above, which concerns cross-*outlet* (not cross-*time*) linkage.

## 14E. PIT specification — hard property of the signal

For every candidate event:

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| `event_timestamp` | The event-date as defined in §14A (effective date, AGM/board-resolution date, or transaction date) — a **date**, not a timestamp; no intraday precision exists anywhere in this corpus (confirmed in Stage 13 §13E: 0% of the corpus has confirmed intraday precision) |
| `knowledge_timestamp` | The article's publication date (§14A publication/knowledge-date definition) — also date-level only, for the same reason |
| `market_session_available` | Per the conservative-boundary rule below |
| `eligible_from` | The **conservative session boundary**, computed by the exact algorithm below — never the same session as `knowledge_timestamp`, since intraday ordering (was the article published pre-market, mid-session, or post-close?) cannot be established from a date-only timestamp. |
| `eligible_until` | Open-ended, until superseded by a restatement/successor event (§14A, §14B) |

**⚠ Correction following independent review (2026-08-08): `events_asof()` does NOT implement this boundary and must not be used to build this dataset without the wrapper specified below.** `src/ngxrot/db.py`'s `events_asof()` — the platform's only existing PIT-read function — filters `announced_date <= sim_date`, i.e. same-day availability. It contains no next-session shift. The original version of this specification stated the conservative boundary in prose but never flagged that no code path enforces it; a researcher reasonably reaching for `events_asof()` to build the GMC/CIR dataset would have silently violated this section's own no-lookahead rule. This is now fixed by specifying the exact, implementable algorithm below, which any dataset-build process **must** apply on top of (not instead of) whatever raw `announced_date`-filtered rows it starts from.

**`eligible_from` algorithm (exact, deterministic, not yet coded — this is the specification a future implementation must follow verbatim)**:
1. Take `knowledge_timestamp`'s calendar date (`D`).
2. Query the platform's own trading calendar — the set of distinct `trade_date` values in `index_levels` **with `confidence >= 0.5`** — restricted to `trade_date > D`, using **any** `index_code` (in practice this platform's index sources share a common NGX trading calendar; if a future index source trades on a different day-set than the others, this step must be pinned to one specific, named `index_code` rather than "any," to keep the calendar unambiguous — flagged here as a condition on this instruction's continued validity, not assumed to be permanently true).
   **⚠ Third correction (2026-08-08, from a second follow-up review): the floor must be checked against the real data, not chosen on principle alone.** A first attempt at this floor set it at `0.7`, reasoning by analogy to other PIT readers in `db.py` that take a `min_confidence` parameter — but a live query of the actual `data/ngx.sqlite` shows `index_levels.confidence` never exceeds `0.5` (distribution: 3,578 trading dates at `0.5`, 1 at `0.4`, 2,736 at `0.0`; the only production writer, `InvestingComProvider`, stamps `base_confidence=0.5` uniformly, per `src/ngxrot/providers/investing_com.py`). A `0.7` floor would have silently returned **zero** trading dates for every query, making every single event's `eligible_from` resolve to `UNKNOWN` (step 4) and excluding the entire dataset without that failure being obvious from reading the algorithm alone — a more severe defect than the unfiltered-query problem the floor was meant to fix. The floor is corrected to `0.5` here: this still excludes the `0.0`-confidence rows (unlabeled/placeholder entries which are the actual thing worth excluding) while admitting the real, currently-used trading calendar. **If a higher-confidence index source is ever ingested, this floor should be raised to match — but it must never be set above what the live data actually contains, and any change to this constant must be re-verified against a live query of `index_levels`, not chosen by reasoning about the schema alone**, per this correction's own lesson.
3. `eligible_from` = the **minimum** such `trade_date`. This is, by construction, the first NGX trading session strictly after the day the information was published — i.e. the session boundary is enforced by reference to the platform's actual recorded trading days (which already correctly exclude weekends/holidays), not by a naive "D + 1 calendar day" shift that could wrongly land on a non-trading day.
4. If no `trade_date > D` exists meeting the confidence floor (e.g. `D` is at or past the end of the currently-loaded high-confidence price history), `eligible_from` is **UNKNOWN**, and the event is held out of the dataset until the calendar is extended — never assumed or extrapolated, and never filled from a sub-floor-confidence row merely to avoid a gap.

**PIT classification, applied per §13E's already-established categories**:
- GMC and CIR events are, by construction (§14A), free of the figure-integrity risk that produced Stage 13's PIT-uncertain earnings-figure category — every GMC/CIR event **is** the disclosed fact itself (a person was appointed; a company was renamed), not a reported number that could later be revised by an audited restatement.
- **PIT-SAFE**: event has a confirmed `event_timestamp`, a confirmed `knowledge_timestamp`, a resolved `eligible_from` (step 3 above succeeded), a resolved ticker mapping, and passes the family's minimum-evidence requirement (§14A).
- **PIT-UNCERTAIN**: any of the above fields is present but not independently confirmed (e.g. an MFA article with an ambiguous or missing in-body date), **or** `eligible_from` could not be resolved (step 4) — **excluded from the dataset**, per the hard-gate instruction that no PIT-uncertain event may enter.
- **PIT-FAILED**: ticker mapping cannot be resolved, or the article fails the minimum-evidence requirement outright — **excluded**.

No event may be assigned `eligible_from` earlier than the value produced by the algorithm above, regardless of how confident the classification otherwise is — this is a hard rule, not a default that can be overridden case-by-case. **Any future implementation that calls `events_asof()` as part of building this dataset must apply this section's `eligible_from` shift as a post-processing step on top of `events_asof()`'s output — `events_asof()`'s own `sim_date` filter is not, by itself, sufficient PIT protection for this specification.**

## 14F. Cross-sectional design

The universe is fixed at the 20 H-011 holdings (`data/reference/stage6_h011_universe_2026-08-08.json`). No artificial continuous score is constructed — both families produce a **discrete signal**:

- **GMC**: `direction ∈ {+1 (bullish), 0 (neutral/unknown), -1 (bearish)}`, per §14C's objective rule table. As disclosed in §14C, the current in-sample GMC events all resolve to `0`.
- **CIR**: same `{+1, 0, -1}` representation, per §14C's CIR rule table.

**Explicit handling of edge cases** (all required by the instruction, none deferred):
- **Simultaneous events** (two different qualifying events for the same ticker on the same `announced_date`): both are retained as **separate rows** with distinct `canonical_event_id`s (they have different `event_type`s or different structural-change descriptions, so the natural key does not collide) — never merged or averaged.
- **Multiple events for one ticker over time** (e.g. UNIVINSURE's CEO confirmation in 2025 and its later, unrelated suspension in 2025-09 — the latter out of scope for GMC/CIR but illustrating the general principle): each retained as its own row; no ticker-level aggregation or netting is performed at this stage.
- **Conflicting events** (two sources disagree on direction/effective_date for what appears to be the same event): handled by the existing `validate_batch()` `CONFLICT` path (§14D) — both preserved, never resolved by deletion, and excluded from the eligible dataset until a research process explicitly resolves the conflict (marked `PIT_status=PIT-UNCERTAIN` in the interim).
- **Repeated events** (the same individual/company undergoing the same event_type more than once, e.g. a second chairman change at the same company later in time): each is a **distinct** event with its own `canonical_event_id`, not a repetition of the first — no special-casing needed since the natural key already differentiates by `announced_date`.
- **Event clustering** (multiple qualifying events for different tickers on the same calendar date — a real possibility given regulatory-deadline-driven timing observed in the recapitalization family, though that family is out of scope here): no cross-ticker aggregation, weighting, or clustering adjustment is applied; each ticker's event stands alone in the cross-section.
- **Missing observations** (a ticker with zero qualifying GMC/CIR events in a given research window): represented as **no row**, never as an implicit `0`/neutral row — a missing observation is not the same as a confirmed-neutral observation, and conflating the two would silently misrepresent "no news" as "neutral news." Any research process consuming this dataset must treat ticker/date combinations with no row as genuinely unobserved, not as zero.
- **No weighting based on historical returns**: confirmed — no field in §14H derives from or is adjusted by any price/return series. The dataset is built exclusively from `events`/`documents` content.

**⚠ Correction following independent review (2026-08-08): a common-cause confound was not addressed and the "INDEPENDENT" label should be read narrowly.** The test above only checks *mechanical input-level* dependency (does H-011's signal construction read or infer GMC/CIR content) — it does not check whether GMC/CIR events and H-011's size ranking could both be driven by a shared, unobserved third factor. A plausible one: distressed or thinly-managed micro-caps could exhibit both elevated forced-governance-turnover rates and small market capitalization simultaneously, producing a correlation between GMC incidence and H-011 membership **without any mechanical link between the two signals**. This platform has not tested whether GMC/CIR event incidence varies systematically by size decile within or near the IRU. Until that check is run, **INDEPENDENT** in the table above should be read specifically as "no mechanical input-level dependency" — a necessary but not sufficient condition for the signals to be usable as a genuinely independent second alpha source. This is an open risk, not a blocker to freezing the event/direction definitions in §14A/§14C, but it must be checked (event-incidence-by-size-decile, using only pre-existing size data, no new event collection required) before any H-019 result is treated as clean of H-011.

It established that `source_id=16` had covered both approved outlets since Stage 10C and could never distinguish them, and that 4 Stage 12 `management_change`/`capital_raise` events (event_id 178–181) had no linked `documents` row. Remediation: §14D's "fifth correction" (above) replaced the `source_id` cross-outlet test with a `source_url`-domain-based one; the 4 orphaned documents were backfilled (new doc_id 11552–11555) from evidence already captured in each event row and the real, already-fetched Stage 12 article text; a recurrence-prevention script, `scripts/test_event_document_provenance.py`, was added.

**Round 6** (this correction) independently re-verified the round-5 remediation from scratch — re-querying the database rather than trusting the remediation report's own numbers — and confirmed the backfill, before/after counts, file-immutability, and internal consistency of §14D/§14H/§14I were all genuinely correct. It found two further issues in the *newly-added* material itself:

1. **§14D's normalization procedure specified `urlparse(source_url).netloc`, which includes a port number if present** (`urlparse("https://nairametrics.com:443/x").netloc == "nairametrics.com:443"`), and step 4 only stripped `www.`, not a port — meaning a real, approved-outlet URL carrying a port would have silently misclassified as UNKNOWN. Dormant today (no port-bearing URL exists in this corpus) and fail-closed (under-links, never mis-merges) rather than corrupting data, but a genuine latent defect. **Fixed above**: step 3 now specifies `.hostname` explicitly, with the `.netloc` pitfall stated as the reason, not silently swapped.
2. **`scripts/test_event_document_provenance.py`'s recurrence guard was scoped to `sources.kind='web_archive'`**, a schema-enforced enum a future news source is not guaranteed to use (the platform's own `investing_com` source, for comparison, uses `kind='vendor'` for a non-news purpose) — a plausible future registration choice could silently evade the guard. **Fixed separately in the script itself** (not in this spec document) — see the script's own updated docstring/logic, which now also matches on the approved-outlet domain allow-list (the same one just defined in §14D) as a second, independent detection path, rather than relying on `sources.kind` alone.

No event, direction, PIT, or dataset-schema definition in §14A/§14C/§14E/§14H was reopened by this round. The correction was confined to §14D (this document) and to the standalone test script (a separate file, not part of this specification).
