## 9. Challenging the platform's own failed/frozen hypotheses first (negative evidence, not retroactive optimization)

**H-011 (Size)**: confirmed as a hypothesis by the validation gauntlet (placebo, stability, OOS), but Stage 1's own execution-realism work — done on this platform, not imported — found the universe severely capacity-constrained (near-total leg rejection at realistic institutional AUM under the standard 10%/60-day ADTV cap; H-019's own backtest reproduced the identical finding independently, §12 of the H-019 result doc). **Lesson**: an apparent premium in NGX's smallest/most illiquid names may be real in an unconstrained backtest and largely uncapturable once realistic execution frictions are applied. Any new hypothesis built on this same universe inherits this constraint by default, not as a surprise to discover later — capacity must be assessed *before* a hypothesis is taken seriously, not after.

**H-019 (GMC/CIR news events)**: passed every integrity gate (PIT, dedup, provenance, mechanical independence from H-011) — and then produced 2 realized trades, both losses, both worse than the benchmark. Two things are worth extracting as *evidence about the market*, not as reasons to touch the frozen result: (a) §14C's direction rule, applied honestly, resolved 7 of 9 non-executable events to NEUTRAL *by design* — a governance/personnel-change disclosure, even perfectly extracted, usually carries very little unambiguous directional content on its own. That may be a real property of this information class on NGX, not a sampling artifact. (b) A finding from this stage's own regulatory search (§A below): **DEAPCAP — one of H-019's two executable, "positive"-classified events — was on NGX's own Delisting Watch List as of March 2026**, the same period as its capital-injection/rename event and its subsequent large realized loss. This was never visible to the single-article FSI extraction that generated H-019's DEAPCAP row. **Lesson, stated as a general finding, not a fix to H-019**: single-document FSI extraction can be fully grounded and accurate to its source and still miss company-level distress context that a broader, independent cross-reference (a delisting watchlist, a recapitalization-compliance list) would have surfaced. This is a real, generalizable methodological finding for *future* hypothesis construction — cross-referencing structured "risk-flag" registries alongside event extraction — not a retroactive edit to H-019's frozen dataset.

## B. Information-mechanism map (causal chain, not "good news → up")

| Mechanism | Chain: information → participants → constraint → behavior → price → persistence |
|---|---|
| **Bank/insurance recapitalisation** (A1/A2) | Regulator sets hard capital floor → capital-deficient institutions must raise equity or exit → dilutes existing shareholders proportional to new-share issuance → market must reprice per-share economics, not just headline compliance → **persistence question**: does the market correctly price *dilution magnitude* differences across institutions, or react uniformly to "recapitalised = safe" regardless of how much dilution occurred? |
| **PenCom equity-allocation increase** (A6) | Regulator raises the ceiling on how much of ₦(multi-trillion) in pension assets *may* sit in equities → pension fund managers, subject to their own internal risk/liquidity mandates, gradually reallocate → likely concentrated in liquid, index-eligible, well-covered names (structural constraint: illiquid names are poor fits for a fund with redemption/valuation obligations) → **persistence question**: is the flow itself slow enough (multi-quarter reallocation, not a single trading day) to be tradable, and does it structurally bypass H-011's small-cap universe entirely (a real risk, addressed in §D)? |
| **NGX free-float rule tightening** (A7, not yet finalized) | If adopted, companies below 20% float relying on the value-threshold exemption must either raise 20% float or exit that exemption → forces a rights issue/secondary offering/block sale by a specific, nameable set of companies → **persistence question**: the forcing event is knowable *before* the rule is finalized, since the affected companies' current float is already public — a genuinely ex-ante signal, contingent on the rule actually being adopted (a real "may not happen" risk to price in). |
| **Delisting watchlist placement** (A8) | NGX places a company on a Delisting Watch List for cause (non-compliance, going-concern issues) → the company faces forced-exit risk, and existing holders face illiquidity/exit-value uncertainty → **persistence question**: is watchlist placement itself already fully priced by the time it's public, or does a name-level "distress flag" carry incremental information beyond ordinary price/volume signals the market already has? |
| **CGT change** (A5) | Government raises corporate CGT 10%→30%, individual CGT moved to progressive bands → rational investors have an incentive to realize gains *before* the new rate takes effect, or defer realization after, depending on their own basis → **persistence question**: this specific change's effective date (2026-01-01) has already passed — any realization-timing effect from *this* change is backward-looking now; the *mechanism* (tax-motivated trading around a known future rate-change date) remains valid for any *future* tax change, not this one. |
| **T+1 settlement** (A4) | Settlement compressed from T+2 to T+1 → reduces counterparty/funding risk, may increase turnover/liquidity market-wide → **persistence question**: this is a market-wide microstructure shift, not a cross-sectional mispricing source by itself — relevant as a *state variable* (H-E) for other mechanisms' persistence, not a standalone signal. |
| **Index rebalancing** (A10) | NGX indices rebalance semi-annually on a fixed calendar → additions/deletions are mechanically determined by cap/liquidity criteria, announced before the effective date → **persistence question, deliberately not assumed**: the MSCI/US-market "index inclusion effect" literature (§ below) requires meaningful *passive AUM tracking the index* to generate real forced buying — whether NGX-30 has material passive-fund tracking (as opposed to being used only as a reference benchmark) is **unverified** and must not be assumed by analogy. |

## F. LLM-value assessment

**Where the FSI genuinely adds value** (information-compression/reconciliation, per §8's own framing — not sentiment):
- **Recap dilution mechanism**: requires combining a regulatory capital *floor* (a number in a CBN/NAICOM circular) with a *specific company's* pre-existing capital position and the *specific structure* of its raise (rights issue vs. private placement vs. asset revaluation — each dilutes differently) — genuinely requires reconciling two or more independent documents per company, exactly the "regulation + company exposure" case §8 names directly.
- **Free-float exposure**: requires combining the (eventual) rule threshold with each company's actual current shareholding structure — same multi-document reconciliation shape.
- **Delisting watchlist**: **does not need the FSI at all** — this is a structured, already-discrete list published directly by NGX; parsing it is a deterministic extraction task, not a reasoning task. Using the full FSI reasoning pipeline here would be over-engineering; a simple structured scrape/parse suffices.
- **PenCom flows**: FSI cannot help — the binding constraint is data *non-existence* (no per-name allocation data), not extraction difficulty.
- **NGX PEAD**: FSI already proven capable of extracting the raw facts (Stages 11–12); the blocker is coverage *depth* (need ≥2 comparable observations per name), not extraction capability — more FSI processing of the *existing* corpus doesn't fix a *coverage* gap, only fresh acquisition does.

## G. Kill list — attractive-looking mechanisms rejected, and why

- **"Bank recap happened, buy the sector"** — dead on arrival: the compliance deadline concluded 2026-03-31, over four months before this report; any ex-ante information content is already fully public and almost certainly already priced. Rejected not because the mechanism was never real, but because its window has closed.
- **"Insurance recap happened, buy the sector"** — same problem, deadline concluded 2026-07-31, 8 days before this report.
- **"CGT change creates a tax-selling signal"** — the specific 2025 CGT change's effective date (2026-01-01) is already past; rejected as a standalone signal for *this* change (the underlying mechanism — tax-motivated realization timing — remains valid for a *future* change, not this one).
- **NGX index-inclusion effect, imported directly from MSCI/US literature** — this is precisely the mistake §11 of the brief warns against. The academic evidence found (2-3% abnormal returns around MSCI EM inclusion) depends on meaningful passive-AUM tracking the index, which has **not been verified for NGX's own indices** and should not be assumed. Rejected as a standalone candidate *until* NGX-specific passive-tracking evidence is found — worth a single bounded search in a future stage, not assumed true today.
- **T+1 settlement as a standalone alpha source** — real and measurable as a *state variable* (liquidity/turnover shift), but it is a market-wide regime change, not a cross-sectional information source; belongs in H-E's toolkit, not as its own hypothesis.
- **PenCom flows as a cross-sectional H-011-relevant signal** — flagged with real skepticism, not rejected outright: pension funds' own risk/liquidity mandates plausibly push flows toward exactly the *large*, liquid names H-011 explicitly avoids (it longs the *smallest* names). If true, this mechanism may be actively **anti-correlated** with H-011's universe rather than a source of incremental alpha within it — worth confirming before investing further effort, not assuming favorably.

## H. Top 5 research candidates

Ranked by the combination the brief specifies (structural plausibility, NGX-specific friction, persistence potential, data availability, PIT integrity, testability) — **not** by imagined performance.

### 1. Delisting-watchlist distress mispricing
- **Single most important experiment**: pull NGX's own published Delisting Watch List and Delisting-in-Process history (as far back as obtainable), cross-reference watchlist-placement dates against subsequent realized returns for the affected names — purely a distress-flag/return-relationship check, no news/FSI needed at all. If watchlist placement shows no measurable relationship to subsequent abnormal returns (i.e., the market already fully prices watchlist status on the announcement day), the mechanism is dead; if a post-placement drift exists, it's the strongest, cleanest candidate of the five because the source is first-party, structured, and requires no article-identity/cross-outlet judgment calls at all.

### 2. Recapitalisation dilution-magnitude cross-sectional mispricing (post-hoc, not pre-deadline)
- **Single most important experiment**: for the now-concluded bank and insurance recap cohorts (A1/A2), compute realized dilution (new shares ÷ pre-raise shares) per institution from NAICOM's/CBN's own published compliance data, and test whether *dilution magnitude* — not mere compliance — predicts cross-sectional return differences among the compliant institutions. This reframes a "dead" macro event into a genuinely untested *relative* question, exactly the "winners vs. losers" framing §6 of the brief asks for.

### 3. NGX-specific PEAD, re-scoped around a genuinely fresh earnings-extraction effort
- **Single most important experiment**: before any signal work, run a bounded, dedicated extraction pass targeting **≥2 comparable-period observations for a meaningful share of H-011's 20 names** (the exact gap that killed Stage 16's H-A) — if that coverage genuinely cannot be built even with focused effort, the track is dead for data reasons, confirming Stage 16's finding rather than repeating it; if it can, NGX's own academic PEAD evidence (real, if dated) gives this candidate the strongest *economic* prior of the five.
- **Explicit accompanying constraint**: does not resolve the unaudited-vs-audited PIT risk that excluded earnings from H-019 — that has to be solved too, not skipped.

### 4. Free-float-rule-tightening exposure watchlist
- **Single most important experiment**: identify, today, which listed companies currently rely on the *value-threshold* free-float exemption rather than genuinely meeting 20% — a static, purely deterministic classification exercise requiring no forecast of whether the rule change happens. This produces a real, ex-ante, dateable watchlist *regardless* of the rule's eventual fate, and can be validated later against whatever the SEC/NGX actually decides.

### 5. PenCom-flow / H-011-universe interaction (explicitly as a risk check, not an assumed opportunity)
- **Single most important experiment**: obtain PenCom's/PenOp's aggregate published sectoral or name-level equity-holding data (to whatever granularity is actually public) and test directly whether pension-fund equity exposure concentrates in large/liquid names — if confirmed, this becomes a documented **structural headwind for H-011 specifically** (worth knowing even though it's not a new alpha source), and only becomes a candidate signal in its own right if some genuinely under-owned but pension-eligible pocket of the universe can be identified.
